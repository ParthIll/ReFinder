Personal statement - Hartwell Foundation First-Gen Scholarship

Every Saturday from the time I was eight, I woke up at 4:30 to help my grandmother open her panaderia on Calle Ortega. My job started small: folding pink bakery boxes, sweeping flour off the floor, counting change for customers who paid in coins. By the time I was fourteen I was running the register on my own and keeping the inventory notebook, a spiral-bound thing with columns of numbers in my grandmother's slanted handwriting.

That notebook is where I first fell in love with problem solving. We were always running out of conchas by 10am and throwing away bolillos at closing. I started tallying what sold each hour and noticed that Saturdays before a holiday looked nothing like a normal weekend. When I showed my grandmother, she laughed and said she already knew that "in her bones" — but she let me adjust the baking schedule anyway, and our waste dropped by almost half over one summer.

Neither of my parents finished high school. My mother cleans offices at night and my father drives a delivery truck. They have never been able to help me with homework, but they taught me something more important: that work done carefully is a form of respect. When I struggled in algebra freshman year, there was no tutor to call. I watched videos at the public library until closing and asked my teacher, Mr. Delgado, so many questions that he started saving me a seat at lunch.

Now I am studying computer science, and I want to build tools that help small family businesses like my grandmother's make better decisions without needing expensive software. Last year I wrote a simple program that predicts daily bread demand from past sales and weather; it is clunky, but she uses it.

This scholarship would let me cut my hours at my part-time job from 25 to 10 per week, which means I could join the undergraduate research lab I have been invited to...
