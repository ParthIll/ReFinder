## Biscuit (THE IMPORTANT PART)

Biscuit is 14, orange, and dramatic. She will act like she's never been fed in her life. She has been fed.

**Food:**
- Morning (around 7–8am): 1/4 can of the wet food (the salmon pâté cans in the pantry, NOT the chicken ones, those upset her stomach) plus a small scoop of dry food in the blue bowl.
- Evening (around 6–7pm): same again.
- Fresh water every day in the ceramic bowl. She also likes drinking from the bathroom faucet; if you're feeling generous, turn it on a trickle for her.

**Medication — please don't skip this:**
- She has an overactive thyroid and takes a tiny pill twice a day, with breakfast and dinner. The pills are in the orange bottle on the top shelf of the cabinet next to the fridge (out of reach because she has knocked them off the counter before).
- Wrap the pill in one of the treat pockets (green bag, same shelf). She's pretty good about eating it if you give it to her FIRST, before her food, while she's hungry. If she spits it out, try again with a fresh pocket. If she refuses twice, crush it with the back of a spoon and mix it into a small spoonful of wet food.
- If you miss a dose, don't double up — just give the next one at the normal time.
- We have exactly enough for the trip plus four extra, so count them if you're unsure whether you gave one.

**Litter box:** in the laundry room in the garage (the cat door is in the door from the kitchen). Scoop once a day, the scooper and bags are on the shelf above. Full litter change isn't needed while we're gone.

**Behavior:** she hides when there's a new person for the first day or so — usually under our bed. That's normal. By day two she'll be sitting on your laptop. She likes being brushed but HATES having her belly touched, no matter how much she presents it. It's a trap.

**Do not let her outside.** If she ever gets out, she usually just sits under the porch looking shocked. Shake the treat bag and she'll come.

**Vet:** Willow Creek Animal Clinic on Halsey, they know her and they have our card on file. If anything seems off — not eating for more than a day, vomiting more than once, hiding and not coming out at all, straining in the litter box — call them and then text us.
